01 / IN THE MOMENT

Before the move.

Championship moves come from a documented public broadcast. Predictions must be published before the observed decision to qualify as prospective evidence.

02 / TWO DIFFERENT QUESTIONS

Choice versus strength.

Did a model anticipate the player’s exact choice? Was that move strong? Exact matching and engine evaluation measure different things.

03 / THE EVIDENCE

Open to inspection.

Our public repository contains the collection protocol, chess rules, tests, and published observations. We disclose gaps before displaying any model accuracy.

02 /

Predictions without hindsight.

Forecasting a grandmaster demands a chronology that survives scrutiny. The live board is an observation tool; the model scoreboard will use a separate, documented evidence standard.

Read the complete protocol ↗
01 / INPUT

Models share a recorded position, legal moves, player identity, prompt, and explicit version.

02 / PRIORITY

A forecast needs independent evidence of publication before the human move. Local timestamps are insufficient.

03 / OBSERVATION

Chess positions are replayed from Lichess PGN. Feed retrieval time is distinct from the physical move time.

04 / DISCLOSURE

No historical test is passed off as a prospective AI result. Engine evaluation and provider provenance are separate verification tasks.
